# The DW1000, as established

What the chip does, as far as it has been established: from the
documents, from the bugs that bit, and from measurement. Not how the
driver is built (`DESIGN.md`), not what was checked against the manual
(`AUDIT.md`), not how to use it (the READMEs): the behaviour itself,
one finding per entry, each graded by where it comes from, so that the
next reader can tell a sentence of Decawave's from a count off the
bench, and a count from an inference drawn over it.

## How to read a grade

Every entry carries a **Source** line naming each ground it rests on:

- **spec**: the User Manual, cited as version, section and page. Page
  numbers are 2.12's unless the version is given; 2.15 and 2.18 are
  cited where they add or change something. The Software API Guide
  rev 2.1 and application note APS022 v1.4 are spec too, and are named
  as such.
- **errata**: DW1000 Errata 1.4 (April 2021), by item.
- **measured**: a date, the host, and a count. The bench is
  ruby-dw1000's: DWM1000 modules on Raspberry Pis (rpi-a, rpi-b,
  rpi-c, rpi-d), SPI through spidev, 6.8 Mbps, 128-symbol preamble,
  PRF 64 MHz, channel 5, unless the entry says otherwise. The
  experiment scripts live in ruby-dw1000's tree or its session
  records, not here.
- **inferred**: a conclusion drawn from the above, marked as such.

Where the grounds agree the entry says so; where the spec is silent,
or says something else, the entry says that instead. None of it is
proof. The manual has been wrong before (the channel 5 values, the
6.8 Mbps SFD wording), and a measurement fixes one configuration on
one bench and can have missed a parameter, so a grade says how the
finding was reached, not that it cannot be overturned. An entry that
contradicts a later measurement should be amended, not argued with.

## Transmit

### A transmit start written into an enabled receiver is dropped

**Source**: measured 2026-09-18, rpi-d, 200 of 200; spec silent.

Two hundred raw `TXSTRT` (buffer and `TX_FCTRL` written, no `TRXOFF`
first) into a receiver enabled with nothing on the air: two hundred
dropped, no `TXFRB`, no `TXFRS`, the chip still receiving,
`SYS_STATE` reading `0x4x050500` before the write and PMSC 5
immediately after it. The same signature was seen fourteen times on
the sends lost under RXAUTR (below), each a start written where the
receiver had come back up.

The manual's `TXSTRT` text (2.12 §7.2.15, p. 80) opens "When the
DW1000 is in idle mode and the TXSTRT bit is set", and says nothing of
any other mode; the API guide's `dwt_starttx` (§5.15, p. 27) says
nothing of the receiver's state either. So this fills a gap rather
than contradicting anything: the precondition the manual assumes is a
real one, and the chip enforces it by ignoring the command.

### TRXOFF aborts a transmission in flight, and no completion follows

**Source**: spec 2.12 §7.2.15, p. 81; measured indirectly, 2026-09-17.

"When this is set the DW1000 returns to idle mode immediately. Any TX
or RX activity that is in progress at that time will be aborted." The
bench showed the consequence rather than the act: a host that handled
a stale receive error with `TRXOFF` after writing a send never saw
`TXFRS` for it, the larger part of the original one loss in a thousand
on the two-node soaks (the RXAUTR entry below being the rest). The
abort itself is the manual's word; the "no `TXFRS`" is what the soaks
saw.

### The transmit event flags clear at the next transmitter enable, which for a delayed send is the programmed instant

**Source**: spec 2.12 §7.2.17, pp. 86–87; measured 2026-09-18, rpi-d,
20 of 20.

Each of `TXFRB`, `TXPRS`, `TXPHS` and `TXFRS` "is automatically
cleared at the next transmitter enable. It can also be cleared
explicitly by writing a 1 to it." The manual does not say whether a
delayed send's enable is the `TXSTRT` write or the `DX_TIME` instant.
Measured: a delayed start with a 5 ms lead armed over a standing
`TXFRS`, with no explicit clear, kept the flag set at the command, at
2 ms and at 4.5 ms into the wait, and had it set again at 9 ms, after
the send went out; `TXFRB` the same. So the clear happens when the
transmitter actually starts, and a host reading the status word during
the wait sees the previous send's flags.

### While a delayed send waits, the chip is in a state of its own

**Source**: measured 2026-09-18, rpi-d, 20 of 20 at three instants;
spec silent in 2.12.

`SYS_STATE` reads `0x00020000` (PMSC 2, TX 0, RX 0) at 0.5, 2 and
4 ms into a 5 ms wait, every time. See the `SYS_STATE` entry for the
register.

### The delayed-send lead has a floor, and it is the preamble airtime plus a host constant

**Source**: spec APS022 v1.4 (Ton), 2.12 Table 60; measured
2026-09-15, rpi-a, four preamble lengths, three runs each.

The smallest lead the chip accepts against the airtime computed from
Table 60:

| preamble | Ton computed | min lead measured | residual |
| :------- | -----------: | ----------------: | -------: |
| 128  | 138.4 µs  | 173.4 µs  | 35.0 µs |
| 256  | 268.7 µs  | 303.4 µs  | 34.7 µs |
| 1024 | 1050.2 µs | 1085.1 µs | 34.9 µs |
| 4096 | 4176.3 µs | 4211.3 µs | 34.9 µs |

The floor tracks the preamble over a thirtyfold range while the
residual stays flat to 0.3 µs, so the floor is the preamble and SFD
airtime and not a transmit power-up, which would not scale. The
residual is the host's register writes and whatever power-up there is
together, about 35 µs on a Raspberry Pi; this measurement cannot
separate them.

### Errata TX-1: a delayed send just above the too-late boundary is accepted and never happens

**Source**: errata TX-1; measured on rpi-b (ruby-dw1000
`measurements/errata-tx1/README.md`), 200 attempts per lead at
0.1 µs steps, 85 silent losses in 3347 eligible attempts before the
workaround, 0 in 3279 after.

The band is about 0.4 µs wide, immediately above the lead at which the
chip stops refusing the send as too late (`HPDWARN`), with the loss
rate falling 85.7 %, 52.7 %, 18.3 %, 4.9 %, 0 across 159.2 to 159.6 µs
on that host. The erratum's workaround, forcing the TX clock on for a
delayed send, removes the band entirely at that resolution; it costs
about 8.5 µs of extra lead on rpi-b, nearly all of it spidev overhead.

Whether a band survives inside the reported region was measured on
2026-09-18, rpi-b, with the driver keeping the two flags it saw at the
refusal (`tx_late_flags`, a `DW1000_WITH_DEBUG` diagnostic): 1240 attempts at 1 µs steps
from 150 to 180 µs and 7200 at 0.1 µs steps from 172 to 175.5 µs, 0
lost, every refusal carrying a flag. The region has a structure: below
168 µs every refusal is `HPDWARN`; from 168 to about 173.4 µs every
refusal is `TXPUTE` (the transmitter has the time but not the power-up,
APS022 §5.4) with the odd `HPDWARN` from host jitter; above that the
sends go through, 173.5 µs being where half of them do. No refusal
came unflagged and no send went missing, so the band does not survive
in either form. The boundary sits 5.7 µs later than the 167.8 µs of
the 1.3.0 measurement on the same host: the status write that
`dw1000_tx_start()` now makes before a delayed send is one more spidev
transaction.

### The counter gains at a delayed send's launch, not during its wait, and not because of the TX clock

**Source**: measured, ruby-dw1000 `measurements/delayed-send/README.md`
and `measurements/errata-tx1/README.md`, two runs each; the cancelled
control 2026-09-18, rpi-b to rpi-d, 150 cycles at four leads.

Across a delayed send the sender's counter gains 2.5 to 3.4 ppm of
the lead (the figure is the chip's: 0.4 to 0.65 ppm on the other two
modules), with `TXCLKS` forced on or not, so the TX-1 workaround
neither causes nor cures it. The same send armed and taken back by
`TRXOFF` a millisecond before its instant gains 0.3 ns whatever the
lead, against 5.4 to 46.1 ns when it fires: the gain is not accrued
while the send waits, it comes with the launch. What the chip does at
that instant is unexplained here; the manual gives only the 8 ns grid
of `DX_TIME`.

### TXPUTE means the frame is not on time

**Source**: spec APS022 v1.4 §5.4; 2.12 §7.2.17 reads otherwise.

2.12's `TXPUTE` text can be read as "the frame would still go out on
time"; APS022 §5.4 requires the send be aborted on it. Settled for
APS022 in the audit (see `AUDIT.md`, "Refuted candidates"); not
measured.

### Manual transmit power has a 2.5 dB coarse step

**Source**: spec 2.18 §7.2.31.1; 2.12 says 3 dB.

The later manual corrects the coarse gain step, which moves the
encoding's clamp and step. Not measured; adopted from 2.18.

## Receive

### RXENAB takes 16 µs to reach the preamble hunt

**Source**: spec 2.12 §7.2.15, p. 82; measured 2026-09-18, rpi-d,
295 of 300 first reads, consistent.

"The receiver has a delay of 16 µs after issuing the enable receiver
command, after which it will start receiving preamble symbols."
`SYS_STATE` read as soon as the SPI transaction after `RXENAB` allows
shows PMSC 3 in 295 of 300 cases and PMSC 5 in the other five; at
100 µs it is 5 in all 300. PMSC 3 is therefore the start-up state, and
the spec's figure is consistent with the reads, though a Pi's SPI
cadence cannot resolve 16 µs.

### RXAUTR re-enables the receiver after a receive error, in every buffer mode

**Source**: spec 2.12 §7.2.6, p. 73; measured 2026-09-17, rpi-c and
rpi-d, 34 sends lost in 243 648 frames with it set, 0 in 440 000 with
it clear.

The manual: with `RXAUTR` set, in double-buffered mode "after a frame
reception event or failure (except a frame wait timeout), the receiver
will re-enable"; in single-buffered mode after a failure only. No
timing is given.

The bench found the consequence for a sender. Two nodes sending at
each other every 30 ms, double buffered, `RXAUTR` set: one send in
about seven thousand written and never begun, 34 in 243 648 frames
over three soaks of twelve runs, the status word at the moment the
host gave up showing no transmit flag and `SYS_STATE` RX. `RXAUTR`
clear, same driver, same bench: none in 440 000 frames over six soaks,
receive counts unchanged.

**Inferred** mechanism: the `TRXOFF` before a send cuts a reception
in progress, the chip raises the error, the re-enable brings the
receiver back before the frame write over SPI is over, and the
`TXSTRT` goes into an enabled receiver (first entry). The re-enable's
latency is not measured directly; what is measured is that the start
finds the receiver up, fourteen sightings, and that the losses vanish
with the bit clear.

### RXAUTR is what makes a stalled receiver overrun

**Source**: measured 2026-09-17, rpi-d, 0 overruns of a 3000-frame
flood with it clear against 2 with it set; consistent with spec 2.12
§7.2.6.

A receiver stalled on purpose for 0.5 s under a flood: with `RXAUTR`
clear the chip holds the first frame, idles after the second, and the
third never lands, so `RXOVRR` never rises. With it set the receiver
re-enables after each frame and the overrun comes. The manual's (a)
above predicts this; the count confirms it.

### An overrun is recoverable, and recovery is a receiver reset

**Source**: spec 2.12 §4.3.5, p. 39, and §7.2.50.1, p. 196; measured
2026-09-16, rpi-d, 3590 of 4000 flooded frames delivered after the
first of three overruns.

"A receiver-only reset must be applied to the receiving device to
clear the errored state which may persist"; `SOFTRESET` bit 28 is the
reset. Measured on a quiet channel with the flood paced so its frames
reach the air: three overruns, 3590 frames received after the first,
every one carrying the flood's own payload. The receiver recovers as
documented. (The test that once reported it deaf had two faults of
its own; see the bench entries at the end.)

### After RXPHE, RXRFSL, RXRFTO the receiver must be reset for the next timestamp to be right; not after RXPTO or RXSFDTO

**Source**: spec 2.12 §4.1.6, p. 34; not measured.

"Due to an issue in the re-initialisation of the receiver, it is
necessary to apply a receiver reset after certain receiver error or
timeout events (i.e. RXPHE, RXRFSL, RXRFTO, etc.). This ensures that
the next good frame will have correctly calculated timestamp. It is
not necessary to do this in the cases of RXPTO and RXSFDTO." The
timestamp corruption itself has not been provoked on the bench; the
driver resets as told.

### A receiver enabled after a completion is honoured, at fifty microseconds and beyond

**Source**: measured 2026-09-18, rpi-d, 300 of 300 twice; spec silent.

Three hundred sends, the completion polled over SPI and `RXENAB`
written the moment it appeared, some fifty microseconds after the end
of the frame at that cadence: `SYS_STATE` RX at a hundred microseconds
and at a millisecond, and a frame from the peer heard within 40 ms,
every time. Three hundred more with the completion booked before the
enable, the same. The manual states no delay before `RXENAB` or
`TXSTRT` is accepted after `TXFRS` or `TRXOFF` (2.12 §7.2.15, §2.3.2
Table 1), and the one transition it does time, `TRXOFF`, it calls
immediate. Not excluded: a window under fifty microseconds, which an
event pass can reach when the completion is in the status word it has
just read (see "Unexplained").

### A single-buffered receiver is deaf while its frame is read out

**Source**: spec 2.12 §2.3.2, p. 17, by implication; measured
2026-09-17, rpi-c and rpi-d, 0 of 240 against 240 of 240.

Table 1 has the chip return to IDLE after any event that ends a
reception, so a single-buffered receiver reading a frame out over SPI
is not listening. Measured with the probe's three-frame exchange
(POLL, REPORT, FINAL), four rounds round-robin, 30 exchanges each:
with a double-buffered responder 120 of 120 resolved from either
initiator; with a single-buffered responder 0 of 120 from either, every
failing record showing POLL and FINAL heard and the REPORT never seen,
no drop counted anywhere. The third frame fell in the read-out window.
Deterministic at that cadence, not intermittent.

### With the buffer pointers aligned, the swinging status bits are the chip's flags, not the buffer's

**Source**: measured 2026-09-18, rpi-c and rpi-d, every event pass of
144 duplex runs traced with `SYS_STATUS` read on entry, after the
masked clear and after `HRBPT`; spec 2.12 §4.3.3 describes the
swinging set and says nothing of this.

Double buffered, `RXAUTR` clear. Three things the traces show, each
on every pass of its kind:

- With the receiver enabled (the driver writes `RXENAB` before it
  reads a frame out), the masked clear of `RXDFR|RXFCG|LDEDONE` for the
  host's buffer does not take: the bits read back set, and disappear
  only with the toggle, which moves the host onto the buffer the chip
  is hunting on. The single-instance bits `RXPRD|RXSFDD|RXPHD` clear
  as written.
- With the receiver off (a frame read out over this node's own send,
  or beside its completion), the same clear takes.
- After a toggle that leaves `HSRBP == ICRBP` with the receiver off,
  the swinging bits read as `RXFCG|RXDFR|LDEDONE` again, with
  `RXPRD|RXSFDD|RXPHD` clear; a masked clear written there reads back
  set; the receive registers select the host's buffer, which holds the
  frame before the one just read. The next receiver enable resets
  them.

**Inferred**: with the pointers on one buffer what the host reads is
the chip's own record of its last reception, which a status write
cannot clear and a receiver enable does; a buffer's latched set is
what the host sees while the pointers differ. The driver, which read
the frame the pointers agreed on, delivered the previous frame a
second time with the flags of the last one: one reception in about
300 in the duplex soaks, 31 in 96 runs, every one after a frame read
out with the receiver off. It now takes `RXFCG` with the pointers
aligned and none of `RXPRD|RXSFDD|RXPHD` for what it is, reports
nothing and toggles nothing, and leaves the enable that ends the pass
to reset the flags: 0 duplicates in 48 runs against 13 and 11 in the
two before, 78 such passes recognised, nothing else changed.

### Double buffering shifts the measured distance by about two centimetres on the initiator side

**Source**: measured 2026-09-17, same runs as above; the 6 to 10 cm
figure remembered from earlier is not reproduced.

Initiator double buffered against single, responder double buffered
in both, n = 120 each: the asymmetric estimator moves +23.0 mm at
9.7 sigma, consistent across all four rounds (+32.6, +18.4, +18.9,
+22.3 mm) and tighter double buffered (sd 16.2 against 20.3 mm); the
symmetric estimator +17.6 mm at 1.7 sigma; the single-sided one
−48.9 mm at 2.7 sigma. The three do not agree on a sign, so "the
bias" is not one number, and the responder cannot be varied because a
single-buffered responder resolves nothing. One pair, one geometry,
one session.

## The status and state registers

### SYS_STATE (0x19), reserved in 2.12, reads the transceiver's state

**Source**: measured 2026-09-17 and 18, rpi-c and rpi-d, counts below;
spec 2.12 §7.2.27, p. 103 has the register file "reserved"; 2.18
§7.2.27, pp. 98–100 documents its fields and APS022 v1.4 §4.3–4.6,
pp. 11–12 tabulates them; compared 2026-09-18, and every reading
agrees with the documented name.

Low 32 bits: `TX_STATE` bits 3..0, `RX_STATE` bits 11..8,
`PMSC_STATE` bits 23..16 (2.18; the driver masks five bits of it,
which the values so far fit), bits 31..24 reserved:

| Reading      | When                                              | 2.18's name | How many |
| :----------- | :------------------------------------------------ | :---------- | :------- |
| `0x00010000` | idle                                              | PMSC `IDLE` | routine |
| `0x00020000` | a delayed send waiting for `DX_TIME`, 5 ms lead   | PMSC `TX_WAIT`, "waiting to start transmitting" | 20 of 20, at 0.5, 2 and 4 ms |
| PMSC 3       | the first microseconds after `RXENAB`             | PMSC `RX_WAIT`, "waiting to enter receive mode" | 295 of 300 first reads |
| `0x00040001` | a frame going out, `TX_STATE` 1 (preamble)        | PMSC `TX`, TX `PREAMBLE` | 47 647 of 47 661 sends |
| `0x4x050500` | receiver enabled, traffic or none, `RX_STATE` 5   | PMSC `RX`, RX `PREAMBLE_FND`, "waiting to detect preamble" | 200 of 200 idle listening, and every lost send |

APS022 §4.3 gives the same two words as its own example: "if a RX
enable command has been issued, the states register will report
0x050500 (RX) and then once a frame has been received the register
will report 0x010000 (IDLE)". The high nibble varies while listening
(`0x40` to `0x48` seen across the 200 idle reads); it falls in the
reserved byte and neither document names it.

### The latched status bits clear only on a write of 1, except the transmit group

**Source**: spec 2.12 §7.2.17, p. 85; the transmit exception measured
(see Transmit).

"Generally these event status bits are latched so that the event is
captured. Such latched bits need to be explicitly cleared by writing a
'1' to the bit position (writing '0' has no effect)." The four
transmit flags additionally clear at the next transmitter enable.

### Errata SYSSTAT-1: the PLL lock bits are unreliable

**Source**: errata SYSSTAT-1; not measured.

`CPLOCK` and `CLKPLL_LL` cannot be trusted; nothing here reads them.

## Radio configuration

### Channel 5's analogue values changed after 2.12, and the old RF_TXCTRL is a defect

**Source**: spec 2.16 (RF_TXCTRL, Table 38) and 2.18 (TC_PGDELAY,
Table 40, "maximising power in CH B/W"); Decawave's uwb-dw1000 issue 2
(about 2 dB of spurs with the old value); measured 2026-09-16, rpi-c to
rpi-d, three runs a side for power and two of 60 exchanges for
distance.

| | signal power | SDS-TWR distance |
| :-- | -----------: | ---------------: |
| `0x001E3FE0` / `0xC0` (2.12) | −80.98 dBm | 73.2 cm |
| `0x001E3FE3` / `0xB5` (2.18) | −81.59 dBm | 68.6 cm |

Power spread 0.02 dB over the three runs; every new-value distance run
below every old one. **Inferred**: `TC_PGDELAY` sets the pulse width,
pulse width sets the effective antenna delay, so the distance shift is
a bias and every antenna delay calibrated against `0xC0` is off by
roughly 4 cm on channel 5. There is no ground truth in that
measurement; it is a shift, not an accuracy.

### DWSFD selects the Decawave SFD at 6.8 Mbps, and it goes on the air

**Source**: spec 2.15 §7.2.32 ("DWSFD takes precedence over TNSSFD &
RNSSFD"); 2.12 §7.2.32, p. 112 reads as if ignored at 6.8 Mbps;
measured 2026-09-16, rpi-c to rpi-d, 40 frames a run.

| transmitter | receiver | frames heard |
| :---------- | :------- | -----------: |
| DWSFD set   | set      | 40, 40 |
| DWSFD clear | clear    | 40, 40 |
| DWSFD set   | clear    | 0 |
| DWSFD clear | set      | 0, 0 |

Setting the bit changes the sequence on the air: a node with it set
cannot hear one without it, either way round. So the 2.12 sentence
"For 6.8 Mbps the standard 8-symbol SFD" does not mean the bit is
ignored, and the RXPACC adjustment for the Decawave 8-symbol SFD
(Table 18, −10) is the right one there.

### DWSFD with SFD_LENGTH 16 is the 16-symbol proprietary SFD, whatever 2.12 Table 22 says

**Source**: spec 2.15 §7.2.32; 2.12 Table 22 contradicts itself; not
measured beyond the 6.8 Mbps case above.

### The preamble timeout is not a hard deadline

**Source**: spec 2.18 §7.2.40.9; not measured.

### The proprietary long-frame mode is the only way to reach errata RX-1

**Source**: errata RX-1; inferred from the frame ceilings.

Byte 128 of the second receive buffer is corrupted by a transmit write
past offset 127 before read-out. Standard frames stop at 127 bytes, so
the erratum is reachable only with proprietary long frames. Not
reproduced.

## Unexplained, on the record

Kept so that they are not rediscovered as news.

- **One frame in a hundred, by where the enable sits.** With the
  receiver enabled inside the event pass that carries both a received
  frame and the host's own send completion, the scattered loss is
  64 of 4557 frames on rpi-d and 72 of 4672 on rpi-c; with the enable
  at the end of that pass, once the completion is booked, 28 of 4520
  and 49 of 4675 (2026-09-18, 288 runs alternating the two, the
  harness's start skew traced and removed; z = 3.7 and 2.1). The
  enable-after-completion entry says an enable at fifty microseconds
  is honoured; the pass can write one within a few microseconds of the
  frame end, and that window is unmeasured. Real, and the cause is not
  established.
- **The clock-rate excess while a send is pending**, above.

Two former entries here were the bench's, not the chip's, and are
settled in the section below: the "2 of 50 on rpi-c" of 2026-09-17
and the link that seemed to favour rpi-d.

## The bench, for scale

Host-side facts that the counts above are read against; none of them
is the chip's.

- A Raspberry Pi issues about 6 800 gap-less sends a second through
  spidev, 0.15 ms each, against roughly 0.17 ms of airtime for a
  27-byte frame at 6.8 Mbps; a 600-frame flood from one takes 0.24 to
  0.48 s of air. A test that stalls a receiver for 0.5 s must flood
  longer than that (3 000 frames now, 1 900 to 2 400 received after
  the first overrun).
- A host sending gap-less without waiting for the completion loses
  nearly every frame (rpi-d heard 1 of 20 000, 2026-09-16), which is
  the transmit-start entry seen from the sending side: each start
  after the first was written into a busy transmitter.
- Register reads over spidev cost tens of microseconds each, which is
  the resolution every "immediately after" above should be read at.
- **The two-node duplex test did not start its two nodes together**,
  until 2026-09-18. Each burst on its own READY, and the second node
  to come up, some hundred milliseconds to a second and a half later,
  had its collector start that far into the peer's 1.5 s burst and
  missed a contiguous prefix of it. Traced over 98 runs: 299 of the
  355 frames lost were such prefixes, the rest scattered. The "2 of 50
  on rpi-c" was a 1.44 s skew (frames 48 and 49 heard, nothing else);
  the "asymmetry" was whichever node the harness brought up second,
  rpi-c on 2026-09-17, rpi-d on 2026-09-18. The link itself is
  symmetric: 60 of 60 ranging exchanges each way, both chips at 7.5 dB,
  −81.5 and −80.9 dBm received (ruby-dw1000
  `measurements/link-asymmetry/`, 2026-09-18). The harness now says GO
  to both roles once both are listening (ruby-dw1000
  `test/support/remote.rb`, `Job#go`). Every duplex count taken before
  that date mixes link loss with start skew, and the entries above
  that rest on such counts are marked.
- **Two bursts paced from the end of each send drift into each
  other.** With the two duplex roles released together, 70 of 302 runs
  lost the first frames of both bursts at once, up to eleven of them;
  released half a gap apart, the losses moved to frames 20 to 32. A
  send takes a moment of its own, different on each Pi, so "send, then
  sleep the gap" gives the two nodes periods about half a millisecond
  apart, and any starting offset closes in a few dozen frames. Paced on
  an absolute schedule (the i-th frame at start + i × gap) and released
  half a gap apart, 24 runs delivered 2400 of 2400 frames on each node,
  with either receiver-enable placement (2026-09-18). Every scattered
  loss in the soaks before that, about one frame in 300, was a collision
  of this kind; the radio itself lost nothing over that distance.
- **A probe that transmits floods the channel for every node.** The
  errata TX-1 sweep needs no receiver, but its 7200 attempts put 3905
  frames on the air in 1.8 s; run on rpi-b during a duplex soak on
  rpi-c/rpi-d (2026-09-18) it turned one run into 11 and 33 of 50 on
  the two nodes at once. Both nodes low in the same run is the
  signature; a transmitting experiment and a receiving one cannot share
  the bench, whichever nodes they are on.

## Where these are used

`hw/drivers/dw1000/DESIGN.md` says how the driver is built on them,
